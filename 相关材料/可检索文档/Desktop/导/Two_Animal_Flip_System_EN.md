# PART 1 — ANIMAL RECOMMENDATION

## Primary choice: 马 HORSE

**Why it is the strongest possible second animal**

1. **The brand is 悲鸿.** Xu Beihong's single most famous subject is the
   galloping horse — 奔马. A horse plate is not decoration, it is the
   house signature. The brand already carries the name; the horse completes it.
2. **A gallop is the ideal two-frame subject.** One frame = all four legs
   gathered, body compressed. The other = legs extended, body stretched.
   Alternating those two reads instantly as speed.
3. **It scales to a sequence.** If you later want 4 or 6 frames instead of 2,
   a horse gallop breaks into a clean cycle naturally.
4. **You already gathered horse references** (`用户风格参考_群马.png`).

## Alternative choice: 松鼠与板栗 SQUIRREL + CHESTNUT

**Why it is the best locally-grounded option**

1. **Chestnut is Dongtai Village's signature crop.** The earlier Codex round
   already produced 「共印与栗影」. A squirrel with a chestnut is content the
   village actually owns.
2. **A two-frame subject too:** frame A = squirrel upright, tail curled;
   frame B = squirrel leaning down, tail flicked. Reads as a scurry.
3. **It carries the season** — chestnuts ripen in early autumn, exactly when
   the leadership visit happens.

# PART 2 — SCENE INTEGRATION (场景化)

The current flip piece is an animal alone on a blank card. The upgrade is to
put the animal **inside the Jinglin scene**, so the animation happens in the
place rather than in a vacuum.

## Layer structure of the finished piece

```
┌─ LAYER 4  the flip piece ────────────────────────────┐
│  A small cut card carrying the two animal frames     │
│  (printed on the new screen, cut apart, hand-flipped)│
├─ LAYER 3  static overprint ──────────────────────────┤
│  悲鸿 YOUNG vermilion mark · 乡村美育工作站 arch mark │
│  Song-serif title and one line of small text         │
├─ LAYER 2  static scene keyline ──────────────────────┤
│  Door frame · lattice window · brick wall · table edge│
│  hanging line with prints · screen frame and squeegee │
├─ LAYER 1  scene halftone masses ─────────────────────┤
│  Deep-green halftone: brick texture, wood grain,      │
│  leaf clumps pushing through the opening              │
└─ LAYER 0  warm paper white — never printed ───────────┘
```

**The trick:** the static layers leave an **empty aperture** — the open door,
the window, or the stretch of courtyard between the door and the table.
The flip card sits in or over that aperture, so flipping the animal makes it
**fly out of the doorway** or **gallop across the yard**.

## Three scene placements (pick one per animal)

| Scene | Aperture | Effect when flipped |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| **A. The Doorway** | the open timber door | the animal flies / runs out of the workstation |
| **B. The Window** | the lattice window | the animal passes behind the lattice — a framing device |
| **C. The Yard Line** | the open stretch between table and door | the animal crosses the yard in one stride |

Recommended pairing: **bird → The Doorway**, **horse → The Yard Line**,
**squirrel → The Window** (the lattice gives it a place to clamber).

# PART 7 — CRAFT KIT FOR THE VISIT (unchanged from v20, keep it)

Prepare: A4 base sheets, deep-green water-based screen ink, a screen,
a squeegee, scissors, narrow tape, a pencil.

- Print the template sheet first, align the bird shape against the screen image
- Set two registration stops against the A4 paper edge
- Swap in the blank base sheet, print, check the animal position, then batch
- Block out the frame text, registration crosses and test line with tape
  before inking — they are not part of the artwork
- Order of the demo: show the pre-made flapping sample → let the visitor print
  the animal layer themselves → show how two frames become motion → cut and
  assemble once dry. If the ink is still wet, demo with a pre-made card.

**Demonstration line:**
> "Both frames show the same animal in two positions. Flip them quickly and
> you see the wing-beat — or the stride. Printmaking, observation and hand
> work come together in one small piece you can take away."

# PART 8 — CONSTRAINTS TO HONOUR

1. **The mentor said twice: do not use generated images; use your own.**
   Use generation for composition studies only; rebuild final artwork from
   the 62 CR2 site photographs, your own prints, and the mentor's references
   (the ram head, the arch logo).
2. **The existing vermilion 悲鸿 screen has not been re-measured.** Do not
   promise arbitrary scaling of the old screen.
3. **Do not call it scanimation.** Two plain frames flipped is a two-frame
   flip illusion. True scanimation needs interlaced bars and a mask.
4. **Two frames printed on top of each other will not animate** — they must
   be separate pieces that alternate.
